Executive summary

☑ Two tuning changes applied (2026-06-08), one lever each:
• Monster attack curve softened L^1.5→L^1.25 (gen_monster_curves.py): at L100 monster attack 1000→316. A same-level Staff's touch hit drops from 755 (29% HP, died in 3) to 171 (7% HP, dies in 15) — MapleStory chip damage, no more potion-spam at-level. Armor matters MORE per point (mitigation rises as attack falls), and player damage is untouched.
• Boss health_mult = 10 (ED_EternalWarlord.tres): HP 27,868→557,360, turning a ~12-hit faceroll into a real multi-phase fight (see section C).

Companion to the offense report. Models player HP / DEFENSE / MAGICDEFENSE with the level-appropriate generated armor set per discipline (generate_item_content.gd) against the enemy touch-damage formula (enemy_base.gd.damage_on_overlap), then correlates the monster HP/defense curve to the player's actual ability damage (same combat model as report 1).

⚠ Survivability is wildly asymmetric by discipline because DEFENSE is +1 per STR. At L100 a Sword carries 1,006 DEF (mitigating 81% of touch damage, 56/hit = 1.5% of its 3,807 HP) while a Staff carries only 169 DEF (42% mitigation, 171/hit = 6.5% of its 2,611 HP). The pure-INT mage is a glass cannon vs physical mobs; the sword is a natural tank with zero defensive investment. Per the MapleStory survival model (HP + potions + defensives, no dodge) this is intended direction, but the magnitude is worth eyeballing — a mage must lean on MAGICDEFENSE, kiting, and CON, none of which a sword needs.
⚠ Enemy mitigation of player damage climbs toward endgame. Enemy DEFENSE follows 1.8×L^1.2 (L30≈75, L100≈452), so the player physical def-multiplier falls from ~87% at L30 to ~53% at L100. This is the intended "gear keeps you ahead" lever (gen_monster_curves.py) — fights get longer at high level unless the player upgrades weapons. The tables below show whether that leaves TTK in a sane band.

Methodology & assumptions (click)
  • Player HP = primary-discipline level curve (stats.gd: Sword 120+26/lv, Bow 90+22/lv, Staff 70+23/lv, Dagger 95+22/lv) + CON×8 + armor HEALTH. CON now includes the gear CON folded in (the broad dual-discipline armour gives every piece a CON splash), so even a pure-damage build gets a survivability bump from gear.
  • Player DEFENSE = STR_total × 1.0 (STR→Defense utility) + full armor-set DEF. A pure-primary Sword has a huge STR pool, so its DEF is enormous; a Staff's STR stays ~4.
  • MAGICDEFENSE = armor only (base 0). Arcanist robes & balanced sets carry the most.
  • Armor = the generated set for the discipline's family (generate_item_content.gd): per piece round((base + iLv×slope)×weight), 4 slots (weights 0.6/1.0/0.8/0.5), snapped to the tier ladder at-or-below the character level.
  • Enemy touch damage (enemy_base.gd.damage_on_overlap): mitigation = eff_def/(eff_def + 0.75×att) capped 85%, where eff_def = b×DEF; × level coefficient a = clamp(1 - 0.03×(charLv-mobLv), 0.1, 5); average of the 0.85–1.0 damage roll. b scales DEF down when the player is under-levelled.
  • Your hit = average basic attack (100% damage) through the offense model from report 1 (crit, defense, level-mod, hit-chance). It's a proxy for sustained DPS; the per-ability detail is in the offense report.
  • Enemies: NONE-archetype baseline. Real enemies apply archetype multipliers (Brute 1.6× HP / 1.4× atk, Juggernaut 2.2× HP, Glass 0.6× HP, etc.) and bosses add phases + telegraphed specials that hit far harder than touch damage.

D. Touch-damage reality check vs MapleStory

MapleStory reference: an at-level character in same-level gear face-tanks packs while AoE-farming — normal-mob contact damage is chip you out-heal with the potion loop, and death at-level comes from elite/boss skills, magic, or status, not from walking into a same-level monster. Surviving only 3–4 touch hits from an at-level mob is far harsher than that.

⚠ Touch-damage-to-defense IS out of proportion, and the cause is structural: DEFENSE comes almost entirely from STR (+1/STR), and only the Sword stacks STR. So the Sword self-tanks (969 DEF, 56% mitigation, dies in 8) while Staff/Dagger/Bow are paper (141–371 DEF, 16–33% mitigation, die in 3–4). The mitigation curve def/(def+0.75×att) needs eff_def ≈ 4.25× the monster's attack to reach the 85% cap — at L100 att=1000 that is ~4,250 DEF, which no non-Sword build approaches. Levers (pick one or combine): (1) add a class-neutral DEF source so casters/rogues aren't paper — e.g. CON→DEF, a flat per-level DEF, or steeper armor DEF slopes; (2) soften the monster attack curve at high level (currently L^1.5 to 1000 at L100); (3) make mitigation more generous (lower the 0.75 softness or raise the 85% cap); and/or (4) raise HP pools. Recommendation: combine a class-neutral DEF source with a small attack-curve trim, targeting ~15–30 at-level touch hits to die for squishies and a bit lower for the Sword tank.

E. Magic axis: is magic damage as bad as physical?

Magic enemies (is_magic_attacker) scale on the MAGIC-attack curve (identical magnitude to physical, both now L^1.25) and are mitigated by the player's MAGICDEFENSE — which is armor-only (no attribute feeds it, unlike physical DEFENSE which gets +1 per STR). There are 5 in the game: Stone Slime (L33), Deer Druid (L44), Rabbit Wizard (L48), Fire Slime (L73), Astral Slime (L93).

After the attack-curve softening, both axes sit in a healthy range (~5–8% HP per touch at-level for every class). Magic is NOT meaningfully worse than physical for casters/balanced classes — Arcanist robes and the balanced leather/cloth sets carry MDEF on par with their DEF. The one real asymmetry is the Sword: its physical DEF is huge (STR-fed) but its MDEF is armor-only and Vanguard plate is the weakest magic-resist family, so it takes ~3× more from magic than physical (still only ~6% HP/touch, ~18 hits to die — survivable, just no longer near-immune). That warrior-weak-to-magic shape is classic and arguably intended. Gear DEF/MDEF keep every class in range; no per-piece value is out of proportion now that the attack curve is softened.
