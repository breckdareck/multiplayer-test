Ability Damage & Balance Report

Weapon-identity overhaul · branch pr-7-attributes-class-removal · model mirrors the verified combat.gd / stats.gd formula (2026-05-29)

The damage formula (verified)

max_range = 1.2 × (primary×4 + secondary) × ATTACK_POWER / 100
roll      = rand(max_range × 0.2 , max_range)        # 20%–100% of max
ability   = roll × (damage_percent / 100)            # per hit
per hit   ×= level_modifier(0.5–1.5) × defense_mult(1 − def/(def+500))
if crit (rand% < CRITCHANCE):  × ( rand(1.2,1.5) + CRITDAMAGE/100 )
  • ATTACK_POWER = WEAPONATTACK (melee/bow) or MAGICATTACK (staff). Base 0 — 100% gear-driven; it is the single biggest damage lever. Model assumes ≈ 2×level (anchored to the L100 benchmark ≈197).
  • primary/secondary = base 4 + allocated attributes (5/level pool) + mastery (discipline bonus × mastery level). No per-level auto scaling — it is all manual now.
  • CRITDAMAGE base 0 with no gear source → a crit is only ×1.2–1.5. Crit rate is a near-dead stat.
  • Tables below are raw, same-level, zero-defense single-target to isolate ability balance (defense & level-gap are flat multipliers that hit every ability equally).
  • Ability level & upgrades: each ability scales with character level capped at its own max (so the L100 tables show abilities at max level 20). The per-level tables are base — no upgrades. The Fully-Upgraded Ceilings section below adds the PR6 upgrade trees (best single-target tier-3 variant per ability) to show each ability's true endgame ceiling.
  • Why Level 1 damage is tiny: at L1 the attribute pool is 0 (5×(level−1)) and mastery is 0, so primary/secondary are just their base 4 → stat term = 4×4+4 = 20. With a starter weapon (~9 attack) that is 1.2×20×9/100 ≈ 2 per basic hit. Early damage is almost entirely weapon-gated; it climbs fast as the pool, mastery, and gear all grow together.

Attribute allocation archetypes

The 5-points/level pool (= 5×(level−1); 495 at L100) is spent manually. Three builds modelled:

  • default — discipline ratio (what an un-respecced character auto-gets; e.g. Sword 3 STR : 2 DEX). Reproduces the old baseline.
  • pure — every point into the primary stat. The damage-max build.
  • tank — half primary, half CON (survivability at a damage cost).

Mesh finding · 2 of 4 weapons have no resource shape — everything is a cooldown button

Sword (Combo) and Bow (Momentum) have a full builder → spender mesh and a clear signature moment ("burn 3 combo for the AoE finisher" / "spend Momentum on a Snipe"). Dagger has Stealth payoffs (Opportunist passive +35% dmg, Eviscerate execute) but NO ability enters stealth — the gauge is a payoff with no setup. Staff has no stance / mode / resource at all: Arcane Bolt, Arcane Lance, Glacial Spike, Pyre Burst all behave identically (cast → damage on cooldown). The "+Ice freeze / Fire splash / Lightning chain" tags in the existing model's notes describe an unbuilt design, not the current .tres state. This is where the shape-samey complaint actually lives. Fix Dagger and Staff's resource mesh BEFORE adding new abilities — or those new abilities will inherit the same flatness.

Builds — budget & the dominant picks

The point budget

Each discipline grants 6/mastery level → 120 points at mastery cap (20). Costs: an active maxed (1→20) = 20, a passive (1→10) = 10, full upgrades on any ability = 4 (T1+T2+one T3). So a maxed+upgraded active ≈ 24, a maxed+upgraded passive ≈ 14.

A 120-pt build is therefore roughly 3 core actives (72) + 2 maxed passives (28) + ~20 spare (a filler active or more upgrades). You cannot max a discipline — selective investment IS the build identity. Two weapon slots = two 120-pt pools, but mastery must be earned on each.

  • Attribute meta: pure-primary is +27–43% damage over default with no cost but survivability, so the dominant DPS build dumps all 5/level into the primary stat (STR sword / DEX bow / INT staff / LUCK dagger). CON only for tanky/PvP. Until this is reined in, "what players take" = pure primary.
  • Core actives: players take their highest-ceiling damage abilities — typically the spammable starter (DPS floor) + a DoT-setter + the finisher (Snipe/Eviscerate/Sundering Blow). The big-cooldown utility (dashes, buffs) competes for the 3rd slot.
  • Passives: the primary-stat % passive (Iron Sinews / Fleet Fingers / Arcane Mind / Larcenous Luck) is near-mandatory since it scales the whole kit; HP/defense passives fill the 2nd slot. The +6 passive count means ~3-of-6 is a real choice (good).
  • Cross-weapon loadout: Sword/Bow/Dagger all key off WEAPONATTACK + STR/DEX/LUCK, so a 2-melee / melee+bow pair shares gear & attribute investment. Staff is isolated (INT + MAGICATTACK, separate gear pool) — pairing a staff with a physical weapon splits your itemisation, a real cost. Expect physical/physical loadouts to dominate unless staff offers something they can't.

Balance findings & recommendations

0 · FIX THE ENEMY CURVE FIRST — even-level TTK explodes at high level

Even-level time-to-kill should be roughly constant across levels (an even fight should feel similar at any level). Instead it swings 3.8s → 1.3s → 2.9s → 16.2s (Sword) — enemies become massive sponges past L60. Cause: player damage scales ~quadratically (primary stat × weapon attack, both ~linear in level) while the enemy HP curve scales ~cubically (15 → 1,800 → 130,000), and the defense curve compounds it (def_mult 0.87 → 0.49, so L100 enemies also halve your damage). Recommendation: re-shape the enemy HP curve (and cap the defense curve) so even-level TTK lands ~2–4s at every level BEFORE touching ability numbers. The curves are single .tres files — one lever reshapes all difficulty; far cheaper and safer than re-tuning 30+ abilities to chase a moving target. Early game (L1, 3.8–5s) is the secondary issue — weapon-gated low damage; consider a small base-stat floor or stronger starter weapons.

0b · Bow & Dagger out-DPS Sword & Staff ~2× (low-CD spammables)

Across the TTK table, Bow/Dagger kill roughly twice as fast as Sword/Staff (L100: 8.0s/6.8s vs 16.2s/14.5s). Their best sustained tools are near-zero-cooldown spammables (Snap Shot no CD, Twin Fang 0.5s upgraded) that the model rates very high, while Sword/Staff lean on cooldown-gated hits. Some of this is the DPS-ignores-mana caveat, but the gap is real — verify the spammables aren't free infinite DPS, and lift Sword/Staff sustained floors (or gate Bow/Dagger spam behind mana/Momentum) in the ability pass.

1 · Crit is a dead stat (CRITDAMAGE base 0, no gear source)

A crit only multiplies ×1.2–1.5. LUCK→crit-rate (Dagger primary, +0.1%/pt) therefore barely moves DPS, and the Dagger's Shadowmeld guaranteed crit is worth far less than it sounds. Fix B6: add CRITDAMAGE rolls to high-tier gear so crit builds (and the guaranteed-crit ambush) actually pay off. Until then Dagger's LUCK-primary identity is mostly a non-lever.

2 · Pure-primary allocation ≈ +40% damage over default

Re-speccing all points into the primary stat is a large, always-correct DPS gain with no opportunity cost but survivability. Either soft-cap primary scaling, give the secondary stat a real damage role, or make CON/utility compelling enough to tempt points away. (ADR 0002 Open #1.)

3 · Finishers read correctly as finishers

Snipe (Bow, 200%+10/lvl, consumes Momentum), Eviscerate (Dagger, 190%+10/lvl, stealth execute) and Sundering Blow (Sword, combo spender) top their kits' single-hit damage, gated behind cooldown / a resource. Good shape — the payoff abilities hit hardest.

4 · DoT abilities under-represented in raw tables

Hemorrhage / Barbed Shot / Envenom / Immolate show only their direct hit here; their bleed/poison/burn DoT adds 20%-of-stat/sec ×stacks on top (and Immolate under Fire stance roughly doubles + splashes). Their effective DPS is higher than the table — keep that in mind before buffing their base %.

5 · Spammable starters are low-CD DPS floors — watch mana

Arcane Bolt (1.2s), Twin Fang (1.0s), Steel Flurry (1.0s) and Split Shot (1.5s) deliver high sustained DPS at trivial mana. They set each kit's floor; the cooldown abilities must clear that floor (per-cast) to be worth a button. Most do; verify Cripple (10s CD, 70% dmg) earns its slot beyond the debuff utility.

6 · Cross-weapon parity holds at the starter level, diverges by build

At default allocation the four kits land within a reasonable band. Staff scales on MAGICATTACK (separate gear pool) and leans on its element stance for effective output; Bow's Momentum ramp (+35% dmg / +30% fire-rate at cap) and Dagger's ambush ×2 are multiplicative on top of these raw numbers, so signature uptime — not base % — is where real divergence will come from in play.

7 · Upgrades are a 2.5–3.8× power spike — the real endgame ceiling

Fully upgraded, most damage abilities 2.5–3.8× their base DPS (Twin Fang 3.80×, Cripple 3.25×, Snipe/Eviscerate/Shadowstep ~3.1×). The trees stack three multipliers at once — bonus damage % × extra hits/targets × cooldown reduction — so the "fully invested" ceiling sits far above the per-level tables. Balance the ceiling, not just the base %, or a maxed build trivialises content.

8 · The "+1 hit" tier-3 variant usually beats the "+90% damage" one (single-target)

On 1–2-hit abilities, adding a hit doubles per-cast damage, which beats even a +0.9 damage variant (e.g. Snipe/Eviscerate pick Double Tap/Overkill (+1 hit) over Killshot/Assassinate (+90%)). The flashy big-damage tier-3 variants are traps for single-target — they only win on already multi-hit abilities (Fan of Knives, Twin Fang). Consider rebalancing so the choice is genuinely close.

9 · Low-CD spammables dominate the raw DPS column — finishers are burst, not sustained

Twin Fang (0.5s CD upgraded), Snap Shot (no CD) and Arcane Bolt out-DPS the cooldown finishers on pure sustained single-target because DPS = per-cast ÷ cooldown ignores mana, animation lock, and the fact that finishers are woven into auto-attacks for burst + their signature payoff (Snipe spends Momentum, Eviscerate executes). Don't read the DPS column as "finishers are weak" — read it as "verify the spammables aren't mana-free infinite DPS." Cripple shows the opposite: a 10s CD made it the floor until CD-reduction upgrades (−6s) tripled it — long cooldowns are extremely upgrade-sensitive.

10 · Tune targets, not just numbers

Key starting-value knobs flagged this session: Immolate Fire splash %, Glacial Spike freeze duration, lightning chain hops, Snipe's +12%/stack, Eviscerate's 35% execute threshold, Momentum per-stack ramp. All are isolated constants — cheap to adjust from playtest feel.
